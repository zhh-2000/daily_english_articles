Carbon Pollution Drives Massive Expansion of European Marine Heatwaves

📅 August 24, 2026  |  📖 约950词  |  ⏱️ 建议阅读时间：15分钟

📋 文章摘要

2026年8月，世界天气归因联盟发布研究报告，指出人类碳排放导致欧洲海域热浪"大规模扩张"。部分海域温度比正常水平高出6°C，地中海地区升温约2°C。研究显示，约三分之二的异常热量归因于气候变化，90%的比斯开湾和伊比利亚海岸受海洋热浪影响。热浪导致珊瑚等基础物种大规模死亡，鱼类和海鸟种群遭受连锁冲击，并加剧沿海地区极端降雨。科学家呼吁迅速摆脱化石燃料以保护海洋生态系统。

🌊 Record-Breaking Ocean Temperatures

July 2026 has entered the record books as the month when the world's seas hit their hottest surface temperatures ever recorded. Sea surface temperatures (SSTs) along the European Atlantic coast and in the Western Mediterranean reached unprecedented highs, with some Mediterranean waters recording temperatures as much as 6°C above normal in June. These extremes prompted an international team of researchers from Switzerland, Sweden, the United States, Ireland, and the United Kingdom to investigate the role of human-induced climate change in driving the crisis.

The study, released on August 19 by World Weather Attribution (WWA), analysed four regions that experienced particularly severe warming: the Celtic seas around the British Isles, the Bay of Biscay and Iberian Peninsula (BIP), the Western Mediterranean, and the Eastern Mediterranean. The researchers compared observed sea surface temperatures with climate model simulations to determine how much of the warming was caused by human activity rather than natural variability.

🔥 A "Massive Expansion" Driven by Carbon Pollution

The findings were striking. Carbon pollution has fuelled what the researchers described as a "massive expansion" of marine heatwaves across Europe this summer. Climate breakdown was found to be responsible for approximately two-thirds of the observed excess heat. The Mediterranean warmed by about 2°C due to climate change, the BIP region by 1.4°C, and the Celtic seas by 1.3°C — increases that substantially surpass the level of global average warming in the Mediterranean case.

The scale of the heatwave coverage was equally alarming. Marine heatwaves hit 90% of the Bay of Biscay and Iberian coast region and 80% of the western Mediterranean. In a hypothetical climate not heated by humanity, scientists calculated that these figures would be roughly half as large. Removing the effect of climate breakdown would cut the affected area from 70% to 9% in the eastern Mediterranean and from 80% to 30% in the Celtic region. In the Celtic seas, the heat reached levels that historically struck only once a century.

For three of the four regions analysed, the hottest fortnights of 2026 would have been "virtually impossible" in a pre-industrial climate. The study also warned that in a world 1.4°C warmer — the level expected by the end of this century — these events would be another 1.3°C to 1.9°C hotter, exceeding what many marine species can tolerate.

🐚 Devastating Impacts on Marine Ecosystems

The ecological consequences have been severe and far-reaching. Claire Bergin, a scientist at Maynooth University and co-author of the report, warned that while warmer waters might seem appealing for swimming, the impacts on marine life are deeply serious. Marine heatwaves can lead to the death of habitat-forming species such as corals, sponges, and macroalgae — organisms that provide the structural foundation for entire ecosystems. When these foundation species die, the effects cascade through the food chain, causing mass mortality of invertebrates, fish, seabirds, and marine mammals.

The warming has also driven species range shifts, with marine organisms moving northward into cooler waters. In the UK, fishermen have reported a dramatic increase in octopuses since April 2026, raising particular concern about the displacement of native species. Dolphins have been observed feeding on jellyfish as their usual prey, such as mackerel, declines in availability. Benthic and demersal species, which live on or near the seafloor and have limited ability to relocate, are especially vulnerable to warming and heat extremes, making seafloor ecosystems particularly fragile.

The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC) has warned of an increased risk of water-borne diseases such as vibriosis during sustained periods of high ocean temperatures. Meanwhile, increases in jellyfish abundance — linked to the expansion of low-oxygen zones in which jellyfish thrive relative to fish — have implications for coastal tourism, particularly where high concentrations result in beach closures.

🌧️ From Ocean to Land: Cascading Climate Effects

The impact of marine heatwaves extends well beyond the sea. Because oceans cool more slowly than land, these heatwaves raise dangerous overnight temperatures in coastal regions, worsening heat stress for millions of people. Increased evaporation from warm seas also loads the atmosphere with moisture, which may later fall as intense rainfall events.

Separate research from January 2026 indicated that marine heatwaves lead to more intense rainfall on land. Globally, 5 to 25% of extreme rainfall over coastal land is accompanied by a nearby marine heatwave, with rainfall during these downpours found to be 20 to 30% higher. High Mediterranean temperatures have been linked to extreme rainfall events such as the devastating Valencia floods of 2024, illustrating how ocean warming can fuel disasters far inland.

Jamie Atkins, a scientist at Utrecht University who was not involved in the analysis, noted that the impact of climate change on European seas is "really starting to bite" and should not be underestimated. Mohamed Adow, director of the climate thinktank Power Shift Africa, argued that the findings show "there is no safe corner of the planet in a climate crisis." He criticised the continued expansion of fossil fuel infrastructure, stating that Europe cannot acknowledge the science while continuing to expand fossil fuels, as every new project locks in more warming and pushes ecosystems toward irreversible limits.

🌱 Adaptation and the Path Forward

The researchers emphasised that while adaptation measures can increase resilience and reduce vulnerability, they cannot fully offset the ecological impacts of continued ocean warming. Recommended adaptation strategies include reducing additional pressures on marine ecosystems, protecting critical habitats, adapting fisheries and aquaculture management, and improving monitoring and early-warning systems.

However, the report's central message is unequivocal: a rapid transition away from fossil fuels is essential to save crucial ecosystems, fisheries, food production, and tourism along the European coasts. Europe, as the fastest-warming continent, is increasingly reckoning with the consequences of its own planet-heating pollution. The 2026 marine heatwave serves as both a warning and a call to action — a reminder that the carbon we emit today shapes the oceans that sustain us tomorrow.

🔍 长难句解析

① Carbon pollution has fuelled what the researchers described as a "massive expansion" of marine heatwaves across Europe this summer, with some waters recording temperatures 6°C higher than normal.
结构分析：此句为复合句。主句主语是"Carbon pollution"，谓语是"has fuelled"，宾语是what引导的名词性从句"what the researchers described as a 'massive expansion' of marine heatwaves across Europe this summer"（其中what在从句中作described的宾语，"a 'massive expansion'..."作宾语补足语）。句末"with some waters recording temperatures 6°C higher than normal"是with复合结构（with + 宾语 + 现在分词作宾语补足语）作伴随状语，补充说明温度异常程度。该句通过主句和with结构，完整呈现了原因（碳排放）、结果（热浪扩张）和具体数据（6°C）三个层面。
翻译：碳污染引发了研究人员所描述的今年夏天欧洲海洋热浪的"大规模扩张"，部分海域温度比正常水平高出6°C。
② Removing the effect of climate breakdown would cut the affected area from 70% to 9% in the eastern Mediterranean and from 80% to 30% in the Celtic region.
结构分析：此句为简单句，但结构精炼。主语是动名词短语"Removing the effect of climate breakdown"（消除气候变化的影响），谓语是"would cut"（虚拟语气，表示假设情况），宾语是"the affected area"，后面跟两个由and连接的介词短语"from 70% to 9% in the eastern Mediterranean"和"from 80% to 30% in the Celtic region"作状语，分别说明两个区域的变化幅度。该句通过假设性条件（消除气候变化影响）与实际数据的对比，有力地论证了气候变化在海洋热浪中的决定性作用。
翻译：消除气候变化的影响将使东地中海受影响区域从70%降至9%，凯尔特海域从80%降至30%。
③ When these foundation species die, the effects cascade through the food chain, causing mass mortality of invertebrates, fish, seabirds, and marine mammals.
结构分析：此句为主从复合句。"When these foundation species die"是when引导的时间状语从句；主句主语是"the effects"，谓语是"cascade"（连锁传导，作动词用），"through the food chain"是介词短语作状语；"causing mass mortality of invertebrates, fish, seabirds, and marine mammals"是现在分词短语作结果状语，表示主句行为导致的后果，其中"of invertebrates, fish, seabirds, and marine mammals"是介词短语作定语修饰mass mortality。该句用"cascade"一词生动描绘了生态影响的连锁传导过程，是说明因果关系的典型句式。
翻译：当这些基础物种死亡时，影响会沿食物链连锁传导，导致无脊椎动物、鱼类、海鸟和海洋哺乳动物大规模死亡。
④ Europe cannot acknowledge the science while continuing to expand fossil fuels, as every new project locks in more warming and pushes ecosystems toward irreversible limits.
结构分析：此句为主从复合句。主句是"Europe cannot acknowledge the science"（欧洲不能承认科学事实）；"while continuing to expand fossil fuels"是while引导的现在分词短语作让步/时间状语（while后省略了主语Europe和be动词is，构成while doing结构）；"as every new project locks in more warming and pushes ecosystems toward irreversible limits"是as引导的原因状语从句，其中主语是"every new project"，谓语是并列的"locks in"和"pushes"，宾语分别是"more warming"和"ecosystems toward irreversible limits"。该句通过cannot...while...as...三层逻辑关系，揭示了言行不一的矛盾，是议论文中批判性表达的典型句式。
翻译：欧洲不能一边承认科学事实一边继续扩大化石燃料使用，因为每个新项目都会锁定更多变暖，并将生态系统推向不可逆转的极限。

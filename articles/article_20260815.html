China's "Jiuzhang 4.0" Sets New Quantum Computing World Record

📅 2026年8月15日 📚 来源：Chinese Academy of Sciences / Nature
科技类 · 量子计算

📋 文章摘要

中国科学技术大学潘建伟团队成功研制出可编程量子计算原型机"九章四号"，首次操纵和探测高达3050个光子的量子态，刷新光量子信息技术世界纪录。该原型机求解特定问题比全球最快超级计算机快10的54次方倍，成果发表于《自然》杂志，进一步巩固了中国在量子计算领域的世界领先地位。

A Revolutionary Quantum Milestone

On May 13, 2026, a research team led by Pan Jianwei at the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC) announced a groundbreaking achievement in quantum computing. They successfully developed a programmable quantum computing prototype named "Jiuzhang 4.0," capable of manipulating and detecting the quantum states of up to 3,050 photons—far surpassing the previous record of 255 photons set by "Jiuzhang 3.0."

This achievement was published in the prestigious journal Nature, marking another significant milestone for China in the global race for quantum supremacy. The new system features 1,024 quantum-compressed state inputs and an impressive 8,176 modes, demonstrating a cubic expansion in connectivity compared to earlier versions.

Understanding Quantum Advantage

Quantum computers operate fundamentally differently from classical computers. While traditional computers process information in bits (0s and 1s), quantum computers use quantum bits, or "qubits," which can exist in multiple states simultaneously thanks to quantum mechanical phenomena like superposition and entanglement. This allows quantum computers to perform certain calculations exponentially faster than even the most powerful supercomputers.

The "Jiuzhang" series uses photons—particles of light—to encode qubits. In executing the Gaussian Boson Sampling task, "Jiuzhang 4.0" can generate a sample in just 25 microseconds. By contrast, using the world's most powerful supercomputer and the best classical algorithms would require more than 10 to the power of 42 years. The quantum advantage ratio reaches an astonishing 10 to the power of 54, demonstrating the immense potential of quantum computing technology.

Overcoming Technical Challenges

Developing such an advanced quantum system was no easy feat. One of the biggest obstacles in photonic quantum computing has been photon loss—as encoding circuits become increasingly large and complex, inevitable photon loss severely limits computational capacity. The research team overcame this challenge by developing highly efficient optical parametric oscillator light sources and a spatiotemporal hybrid encoding interferometer.

These innovations allowed them to integrate 1,024 highly efficient compressed-state light fields into an 8,176-mode circuit using spatiotemporal hybrid encoding. This breakthrough represents a major leap forward in low-loss photonic quantum processors, both in scale and complexity.

Implications for the Future

The success of "Jiuzhang 4.0" has far-reaching implications for multiple fields. Quantum computing promises to revolutionize drug discovery, materials science, artificial intelligence, cryptography, weather forecasting, and traffic optimization. The technology could accelerate the development of new medicines, optimize complex logistics systems, and enhance cybersecurity through quantum encryption.

Professor Lu Chaoyang, one of the lead researchers, noted that recent findings suggest "the 'peak' of quantum computing is lower than previously estimated, and our 'climbing' speed is faster than expected." This optimistic outlook indicates that practical quantum computing applications may arrive sooner than anticipated.

Global Competition and Cooperation

Quantum computing has become a key battleground in the global technology race. Countries worldwide are investing heavily in quantum research, with global quantum technology investment exceeding $55.7 billion and projected to reach $106 billion by 2040. The United States, European Union, Japan, and China are all competing to achieve breakthroughs in this transformative field.

China's achievements with the "Jiuzhang" series and "Zuchongzhi" superconducting quantum computers have positioned the country at the forefront of quantum research. The latest breakthrough opens new possibilities for building "trillion-quantum-mode three-dimensional cluster states" and future "fault-tolerant photonic quantum computing hardware."

As quantum computing enters a critical transition period from pure scientific exploration to industrial-scale engineering, international cooperation remains essential. The complexity of developing fault-tolerant quantum computers exceeds the capabilities of any single team, company, or nation, requiring global collaboration similar to what occurred in the microelectronics industry.

Safe by Design: EU Proposes KIDS Act to Shield Children from Online Harm

📅 November 1, 2026  |  📖 约960词  |  ⏱️ 建议阅读时间：15分钟

📋 文章摘要

2026年9月17日，欧盟委员会提出KIDS法案（Keeping Internet Digital Spaces Accountable and Trustworthy），旨在保护未成年人在线安全。法案规定13岁以下禁止使用社交媒体，13至14岁需家长监管且每日限用1小时，15岁及以上方可独立开设账户。法案要求平台实施"安全优先设计"，禁止诱导沉迷的功能，AI聊天机器人对未成年人默认关闭，并将举证责任从监管方倒置至平台方。此举回应了欧洲仅半数9至16岁儿童感到在线安全的严峻现实。

📱 A Growing Crisis

Across Europe, a quiet crisis is unfolding in the lives of children and teenagers. According to research published by the European Commission in 2026, only half of children aged 9 to 16 across the continent say they feel safe online. The risks they face are no longer abstract — they are real, urgent, and growing. Reduced physical activity, increased risk of myopia, disrupted sleep, depressive and anxious symptoms, diminished self-esteem, eating disorders, and addictive behaviours have all been linked to excessive screen time and harmful online experiences. More gravely still, minors face the dangers of cyberbullying, grooming, and child sexual abuse material circulating on the platforms they use every day.

The root of the problem, experts argue, lies not merely in the behaviour of individual users but in the design of the platforms themselves. Social media services, video-sharing platforms, online games, and AI-powered chatbots have been engineered to capture and hold children's attention for as long as possible. Features such as infinite scrolling, reward tricks, auto-play videos, and push notifications delivered during sleeping hours are not accidental — they are deliberate strategies to maximise engagement, often at the expense of young users' wellbeing.

🛡️ The KIDS Act: A Landmark Proposal

On 17 September 2026, the European Commission adopted a proposal that could fundamentally reshape the digital landscape for young people: the EU KIDS Act — formally titled "Keeping Internet Digital Spaces Accountable and Trustworthy." The legislation does two things. First, it sets common age rules for the whole European Union, replacing the patchwork of diverging national laws that currently leaves children in some countries far less protected than in others. Second, it imposes "safety by design" obligations on the digital services that children use most, requiring platforms to be safe from the ground up rather than relying on users to protect themselves.

Under the proposed age rules, children under 13 will not be able to hold social media accounts at all. They may, however, access child-friendly video-sharing services through an account managed by a parent or guardian. Children aged 13 and 14 will be able to use social media, but only through a "mini account" set up and controlled by their guardian — with limited features, a daily time restriction of at most one hour, and parental approval of contacts. Only from the age of 15 will young people be permitted to open and manage their own social media accounts, and even then, the services they access must by law be safe for them.

"Today, our children are engaging with the most sophisticated technologies ever created — technology that was never created with their wellbeing in mind," said Commission President Ursula von der Leyen as she announced the proposal. "Our KIDS Act is reversing the burden of proof: it is for platforms to show they are safe by design. And we put parents back in the driving seat, giving them the tools to help their children navigate a safer online world."

🔧 Safety by Design: Changing How Platforms Are Built

The age restrictions are the most visible part of the KIDS Act, but they are far from its most transformative element. The heart of the legislation lies in its "safety by design" requirements, which compel online services used by minors to offer stronger protection by default. This means platforms must limit or eliminate features that encourage excessive use — infinite scroll, reward tricks, and push notifications during sleeping hours will be prohibited for minors. Profiles for users under 18 must be private by default, unsolicited contact from strangers must be blocked, and easy tools to block or mute other users must be provided.

Perhaps most notably, the KIDS Act addresses the rapidly growing phenomenon of AI companions and chatbots. Under the proposal, AI chatbots and companions must be turned off by default for minors and will no longer be permitted to act in ways that make children emotionally dependent on them. This provision responds to mounting evidence that young users, particularly those who are lonely or vulnerable, can form deep emotional attachments to AI companions — attachments that may displace real human relationships and leave children psychologically harmed.

🔍 Age Verification Without Sacrificing Privacy

One of the most technically challenging aspects of the KIDS Act is age verification. Critics of age-gating have long argued that verifying a user's age inevitably requires collecting sensitive personal data — precisely the kind of data that privacy laws such as the GDPR are designed to protect. The KIDS Act attempts to resolve this tension by requiring online services and app stores to use age assurance tools that protect users' privacy. The European Commission points to its own EU age verification app, launched in April 2026, as a model: the app confirms a user's age without retaining identity documents or biometric data, ensuring that privacy and safety are not traded off against each other.

The legislation also explicitly rejects self-declared age as a sufficient safeguard — a significant departure from current practice, where a simple date-of-birth box is often the only barrier between a twelve-year-old and an account on a platform designed for adults. Under the KIDS Act, access must be gated by certified age verification, and platforms will be required to check existing accounts within six months of the rules taking effect, disabling those belonging to underage users or users whose age cannot be established.

⚖️ Shifting the Burden of Proof

Perhaps the most revolutionary aspect of the KIDS Act is its reversal of the burden of proof. Currently, when regulators suspect that a platform is unsafe for children, they must gather evidence and demonstrate the harm — a process that is slow, resource-intensive, and often outmatched by the legal firepower of big tech companies. The KIDS Act flips this dynamic: providers of very large online platforms will have to affirmatively demonstrate that their services are safe for children and designed with their wellbeing in mind. If they cannot, they will be in violation of the law.

This shift represents a fundamental change in the relationship between regulators and platforms. No longer will children and their advocates have to prove harm after the fact; instead, platforms must prove safety before the fact. It is a principle that could set a precedent not only in Europe but around the world, as governments everywhere grapple with the challenge of protecting young people in an increasingly digital age.

🌍 A Global Conversation

The EU is not alone in this effort. In October 2025, twenty-five EU Member States signed the Jutland Declaration calling for age verification and a safer online environment for minors. Countries including Italy, France, Norway, Greece, Austria, Poland, and Belgium have already proposed or passed national legislation. Beyond Europe, Australia has enacted its own age-verification requirements for social media, and China has introduced regulations governing AI-powered interactive services for minors, including a ban on providing virtual companion services to children. In the United States, several states have passed or are considering similar measures, though federal action remains stalled.

The KIDS Act will now be examined by the European Parliament and the Council of the EU, which will negotiate and decide on the final text before it becomes law. A public consultation remains open until 3 December 2026, inviting input from children, parents, educators, and the platforms themselves. Whatever the final legislation looks like, one thing is clear: the era of leaving children to navigate the digital world largely unprotected is drawing to a close. The question is no longer whether governments will act, but how swiftly and how effectively they can do so — before another generation grows up online without the safeguards they deserve.

🔍 长难句解析

① The root of the problem, experts argue, lies not merely in the behaviour of individual users but in the design of the platforms themselves — social media services, video-sharing platforms, online games, and AI-powered chatbots have been engineered to capture and hold children's attention for as long as possible.
结构分析：此句为并列复合句。前半部分主语是"The root of the problem"，"experts argue"是插入语，谓语是"lies"（意为"在于"），"not merely in... but in..."是not merely...but...结构（不仅……而且……），连接两个并列的介词短语作状语，表示问题根源的双重性。破折号后是对"the design of the platforms themselves"的具体展开：主语是四个并列名词短语"social media services, video-sharing platforms, online games, and AI-powered chatbots"，谓语是"have been engineered"（现在完成时被动语态，engineer此处作动词意为"设计/精心构建"），"to capture and hold children's attention for as long as possible"是不定式短语作目的状语，其中capture和hold是并列动词，"for as long as possible"是时间状语。该句通过插入语、并列结构和破折号展开，层层深入地指出了问题的根本原因。
翻译：专家认为，问题的根源不仅在于个体用户的行为，更在于平台本身的设计——社交媒体、视频分享平台、在线游戏和AI驱动的聊天机器人都被精心设计，以尽可能长时间地吸引和保持儿童的注意力。
② Perhaps most notably, the KIDS Act addresses the rapidly growing phenomenon of AI companions and chatbots — under the proposal, AI chatbots and companions must be turned off by default for minors and will no longer be permitted to act in ways that make children emotionally dependent on them.
结构分析：此句为并列复合句。"Perhaps most notably"是副词短语作评注性状语，表示"最值得注意的是"。前半句主语是"the KIDS Act"，谓语是"addresses"（意为"应对/处理"），宾语是"the rapidly growing phenomenon of AI companions and chatbots"（rapidly growing是前置定语修饰phenomenon，of AI companions and chatbots是介词短语作后置定语）。破折号后是对"addresses"的具体说明：主语是"AI chatbots and companions"，由and连接两个并列谓语"must be turned off by default for minors"（被动语态，by default意为"默认地"，for minors是介词短语表示对象）和"will no longer be permitted to act in ways"（将来时被动语态，no longer意为"不再"，to act in ways是不定式短语作主语补足语），"that make children emotionally dependent on them"是that引导的定语从句修饰ways（that在从句中作主语，make是谓语，children是宾语，"emotionally dependent on them"是形容词短语作宾语补足语）。该句通过破折号解释和定语从句，清晰地阐述了KIDS法案对AI陪伴程序的核心规制。
翻译：或许最值得注意的是，KIDS法案应对了AI陪伴程序和聊天机器人迅速增长的现象——根据该提案，AI聊天机器人和陪伴程序对未成年人必须默认关闭，且不再被允许以使儿童产生情感依赖的方式运作。
③ Currently, when regulators suspect that a platform is unsafe for children, they must gather evidence and demonstrate the harm — a process that is slow, resource-intensive, and often outmatched by the legal firepower of big tech companies.
结构分析：此句为主从复合句。"Currently"是时间副词作状语。"when regulators suspect that a platform is unsafe for children"是when引导的时间状语从句，其中"that a platform is unsafe for children"是that引导的宾语从句作suspect的宾语。主句主语是"they"（指代regulators），由and连接两个并列谓语"must gather evidence"和"demonstrate the harm"。破折号后是对"a process"（指代gather evidence and demonstrate the harm这一过程）的同位语解释："a process"是中心词，其后"that is slow, resource-intensive, and often outmatched by the legal firepower of big tech companies"是that引导的定语从句修饰process（that在从句中作主语，由is和is outmatched两个谓语构成，slow、resource-intensive是表语形容词，outmatched意为"被压倒/不敌"，by the legal firepower of big tech companies是介词短语表示"被大科技公司的法律火力"）。该句通过时间状语从句、宾语从句和同位语定语从句，生动揭示了当前监管面临的困境。
翻译：目前，当监管机构怀疑某个平台对儿童不安全时，他们必须收集证据并证明危害——这一过程缓慢、耗费资源，且往往不敌大科技公司的法律火力。
④ No longer will children and their advocates have to prove harm after the fact; instead, platforms must prove safety before the fact — a principle that could set a precedent not only in Europe but around the world, as governments everywhere grapple with the challenge of protecting young people in an increasingly digital age.
结构分析：此句为并列复合句。前半句"No longer will children and their advocates have to prove harm after the fact"是否定词no longer置于句首引起的部分倒装句（正常语序为children and their advocates will no longer have to...），have to是情态动词短语，prove是动词，harm是宾语，"after the fact"是介词短语作时间状语（意为"事后"）。分号后"instead"是副词，"platforms must prove safety before the fact"是简单句（"before the fact"意为"事前"）。破折号后是对上述原则的评价："a principle"是同位语，"that could set a precedent not only in Europe but around the world"是that引导的定语从句修饰principle（set a precedent意为"树立先例"，not only...but...连接两个并列介词短语）。"as governments everywhere grapple with the challenge of protecting young people in an increasingly digital age"是as引导的原因/时间状语从句（grapple with意为"努力应对"，of protecting young people是介词短语作后置定语修饰challenge，in an increasingly digital age是介词短语作时间/背景状语）。该句通过倒装、同位语和状语从句，有力地阐述了举证责任倒置的深远意义。
翻译：儿童及其倡导者不再需要事后证明危害；相反，平台必须事前证明安全——这一原则不仅可能在欧洲树立先例，也可能影响全球，因为各国政府都在努力应对在日益数字化的时代保护年轻人的挑战。

Question 1: The EU KIDS Act sets 13 as the minimum age for social media access and 15 as the age for independent accounts. Some people argue that age limits are too rigid and ignore individual differences in maturity, while others believe that uniform rules are necessary because platforms cannot assess each child's readiness. What is your opinion? Should digital age limits be flexible, allowing parents to decide when their child is ready, or should governments set one common standard for all children? Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each approach, using examples from the article.
Question 2: The KIDS Act requires AI chatbots and companions to be turned off by default for minors, because they may cause children to become "emotionally dependent" on them. However, some AI companions are designed to help children with learning, mental health support, or social skills practice. Do you think banning AI companions by default goes too far, or is it a necessary precaution? How should society balance the potential benefits of AI for children against the risks of emotional dependency and manipulation? Support your argument with specific reasoning.

What are the potential benefits and risks of requiring companies to prove their products are safe before they are allowed on the market, rather than waiting for harm to be demonstrated after the fact?

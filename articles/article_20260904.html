Deep-Sea Discoveries: Scientists Uncover Hundreds of New Marine Species

📅 September 4, 2026  |  📖 约950词  |  ⏱️ 建议阅读时间：15分钟

📋 文章摘要

2026年8月底至9月初，全球深海探索接连取得重大突破。由海洋生物学家Diva Amon领导的加勒比深海探险队在特立尼达和多巴哥附近海域发现了至少20个疑似新物种和25处冷泉生态系统；澳大利亚研究团队在印度洋偏远海山发现了149个科学新物种，包括新的鱼类、海参和寄生藤壶；西澳大利亚大学利用环境DNA技术在埃克斯茅斯湾识别出487个独特分类群。这些发现不仅揭示了深海生物多样性的丰富程度，也凸显了在气候变化和深海采矿威胁下加强海洋保护的紧迫性。

🌊 A Caribbean Deep-Sea Breakthrough

On September 3, 2026, a Caribbean-led expedition into Trinidad and Tobago's largely unexplored deep ocean revealed at least 25 cold-seep ecosystems, 20 suspected new species, and more than 11,000 square kilometres of previously unmapped seafloor. The month-long expedition, led by marine biologist Dr Diva Amon of SpeSeas, was conducted aboard the Schmidt Ocean Institute's research vessel Falkor (too) from June 29 to July 28.

Researchers mapped approximately 11,335 square kilometres of uncharted seafloor — equivalent to about 13 per cent of Trinidad and Tobago's marine territory. Using mapping data, the team identified 219 bubble streams that can indicate the presence of cold seeps, which are ecosystems fuelled by chemical energy, including methane bubbling from the ocean floor, rather than sunlight. Using the remotely operated vehicle SuBastian, scientists visually confirmed 25 active cold-seep ecosystems, the largest of which was about half an acre and covered in mussels and tubeworms.

Among the suspected new species is an octopus first observed during a 2014 expedition. Scientists also encountered corals, carnivorous sponges, squat lobsters, ghost sharks and zombie worms. In total, at least 54 species never previously observed in Trinidad and Tobago's waters were recorded. However, the expedition also found evidence of human-made garbage, highlighting the reach of human activity even in areas that have never been explored by people.

🇦🇺 Indian Ocean Seamount Discoveries

Meanwhile, an Australian study published in June detailed a separate deep-sea expedition near the remote Christmas and Cocos islands, where researchers visited unexplored seamounts and identified 149 previously unknown marine species. These included black corals, sea worms, sea cucumbers and a variety of fish. The international team of 33 researchers surveyed at depths ranging from 94 to 5,431 metres, mapping a dark abyssal seafloor punctuated by volcanic seamounts that can be up to 70 kilometres across.

Study lead author Tim O'Hara, a marine scientist at Museums Victoria in Melbourne, told Mongabay that these seamounts had never been studied before. "People had been around the islands in shallow water before, but no one had looked at life in the deep. It was a massive hole in the data," O'Hara explained. One of the most intriguing discoveries was a caldera — a bowl-shaped crater that forms after a volcanic eruption — which the researchers named the Eye of Sauron after the character in J.R.R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings, as its centre resembles an eye.

The researchers also discovered approximately 1,000 shark teeth on the Cocos Abyssal Plain, covering more than 22 million years of evolution. The teeth included those of great white sharks, mako, tiger sharks, and even prehistoric Megalodon fossils. At the same site, they found polymetallic nodules — mineral-rich rocks that are highly sought after in deep-sea mining and used in electric vehicle batteries, wind turbines and renewable energy technologies.

🧬 eDNA: A New Tool for Conservation

Adding to these discoveries, an environmental DNA study published on September 1 identified distinct regions of extraordinary marine biodiversity in Western Australia's Exmouth Gulf. Researchers from the University of Western Australia applied a meta-barcoding gene sequencing technique to seawater eDNA samples collected from 19 sites over 3,000 square kilometres, identifying 487 unique taxa, including 440 bony fish, 37 elasmobranchs, seven birds, two mammals and one reptile.

"Exmouth Gulf exhibits significantly distinct marine communities, with the eastern gulf emerging as a hotspot for elasmobranchs and threatened species," said lead author Jessica Pearce. The researchers said eDNA approaches could help identify areas of highest conservation importance to better inform marine spatial planning, offering a non-invasive yet powerful tool for understanding marine ecosystems.

⚖️ Threats and the Case for Protection

Despite these remarkable discoveries, the deep sea faces growing threats from warming oceans, marine heatwaves, overfishing, pollution and deep-sea mining. The Biodiversity Beyond National Jurisdiction (BBNJ) Agreement, which reached 60 ratifications in September 2025 and entered into force in early 2026, represents a transformative step toward safeguarding marine ecosystems in international waters. This landmark agreement, the result of over a decade of international collaboration, aims to establish marine protected areas in the high seas and ensure sustainable use of marine biological diversity.

Jim Barry, a senior scientist at the Monterey Bay Aquarium Research Institute, expressed amazement at the continued discoveries: "I am just amazed that every time we go somewhere and look carefully, we discover so many new species in the deep sea." O'Hara echoed this sentiment, calling the deep sea "the last frontier." He stressed that understanding how the deep sea works is essential, as no one experiences it except a few people who go down in submarines.

🌍 A Global Responsibility

The data from these expeditions could contribute to marine governance, spatial planning, and the establishment of marine protected areas worldwide. Amon emphasised that the findings demonstrate Caribbean scientists can lead exploration of their own deep-sea environments, challenging the historical pattern of foreign-led expeditions. The Trinidad and Tobago expedition collected close to 1,000 water, sediment and animal samples and tested a new cryopreservation technique designed to preserve living animal tissues for future research.

More than 3,000 people were also reached through public engagement activities before and during the expedition, including students, universities, non-governmental organisations and government officials. As oceans face mounting pressures from climate change and industrial exploitation, these discoveries remind us that the deep sea remains one of Earth's least understood frontiers — and that protecting it is not just a scientific imperative but a global responsibility.

🔍 长难句解析

① A Caribbean-led expedition into Trinidad and Tobago's largely unexplored deep ocean revealed at least 25 cold-seep ecosystems, 20 suspected new species, and more than 11,000 square kilometres of previously unmapped seafloor.
结构分析：此句为简单句扩展结构。主句主语是"a Caribbean-led expedition"（中心词为expedition，"Caribbean-led"和介词短语"into Trinidad and Tobago's largely unexplored deep ocean"共同作定语修饰expedition）。谓语是"revealed"（意为"揭示、发现"），其后跟三个并列宾语："at least 25 cold-seep ecosystems"、"20 suspected new species"和"more than 11,000 square kilometres of previously unmapped seafloor"，三者由逗号和and连接。每个宾语都带有前置定语修饰，如"suspected"修饰"new species"，"previously unmapped"修饰"seafloor"。该句通过并列宾语结构，紧凑而全面地概括了探险队的三大发现。
翻译：一支由加勒比主导的探险队深入特立尼达和多巴哥基本上未探索的深海，发现了至少25处冷泉生态系统、20个疑似新物种以及超过11,000平方公里的此前未绘制的海底。
② An international team of 33 researchers surveyed at depths ranging from 94 to 5,431 metres, mapping a dark abyssal seafloor punctuated by volcanic seamounts that can be up to 70 kilometres across.
结构分析：此句为主从复合句。主句主语是"An international team of 33 researchers"，谓语是"surveyed"，"at depths ranging from 94 to 5,431 metres"是介词短语作状语（其中"ranging from 94 to 5,431 metres"是现在分词短语作后置定语修饰depths）。逗号后"mapping a dark abyssal seafloor punctuated by volcanic seamounts that can be up to 70 kilometres across"是现在分词短语作伴随状语（mapping的逻辑主语是team，宾语是"a dark abyssal seafloor"，"punctuated by volcanic seamounts"是过去分词短语作后置定语修饰seafloor，其后"that can be up to 70 kilometres across"是that引导的定语从句修饰seamounts，that作主语，can be为谓语，"up to 70 kilometres across"为表语）。该句通过分词伴随状语和定语从句的嵌套，生动描绘了深海考察的环境特征。
翻译：一个由33名研究人员组成的国际团队在94至5,431米的深度范围内进行了调查，绘制了一片黑暗的深海海底，其间点缀着直径可达70公里的火山海山。
③ As oceans face mounting pressures from climate change and industrial exploitation, these discoveries remind us that protecting the deep sea is not just a scientific imperative but a global responsibility.
结构分析：此句为主从复合句。句首"As oceans face mounting pressures from climate change and industrial exploitation"是as引导的时间状语从句（as意为"随着"，oceans为主语，face为谓语，"mounting pressures"为宾语，"from climate change and industrial exploitation"是介词短语作后置定语修饰pressures）。主句主语是"these discoveries"，谓语是"remind"，宾语是"us"，其后"that protecting the deep sea is not just a scientific imperative but a global responsibility"是that引导的宾语从句（作remind的直接宾语，即remind sb. that...结构）。在宾语从句中，"protecting the deep sea"是动名词短语作主语，is为系动词，"not just a scientific imperative but a global responsibility"是表语（使用"not just...but..."并列结构，意为"不仅是……更是……"）。该句通过as状语从句和not just...but...并列结构，将科学发现与全球责任紧密联系起来。
翻译：随着海洋面临来自气候变化和工业开采日益增长的压力，这些发现提醒我们，保护深海不仅是一项科学上的迫切需要，更是一项全球责任。
④ Cold seeps are ecosystems fuelled by chemical energy, including methane bubbling from the ocean floor, rather than sunlight.
结构分析：此句为简单句扩展结构。主句主语是"Cold seeps"，系动词是"are"，表语是"ecosystems"。"fuelled by chemical energy"是过去分词短语作后置定语修饰ecosystems（fuelled意为"由……驱动"）。其后的"including methane bubbling from the ocean floor, rather than sunlight"是介词短语作补充说明（including引导列举成分，"methane bubbling from the ocean floor"中methane为中心词，"bubbling from the ocean floor"是现在分词短语作后置定语修饰methane），"rather than sunlight"表示"而不是阳光"，与前面的chemical energy形成对比。该句通过分词定语和rather than对比结构，简洁地解释了冷泉生态系统的独特能量来源。
翻译：冷泉是由化学能驱动的生态系统，包括从海底冒出的甲烷，而不是阳光。

Question 1: The article describes how researchers discovered polymetallic nodules — mineral-rich rocks used in electric vehicle batteries and renewable energy technologies — at the same site where they found 1,000 shark teeth covering 22 million years of evolution.

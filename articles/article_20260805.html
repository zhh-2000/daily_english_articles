AI Powers China's Education Transformation Drive

China Daily | August 4, 2026
科技与教育

📌 文章摘要

本文报道了中国将人工智能融入基础教育的变革举措。通过AI智能"代理"分析学生数据、生成个性化作业，教师得以从繁重的批改工作中解放出来，将更多时间投入到教学设计和个性化指导。文章介绍了北京多所学校的实践案例，展现了AI如何助力实现从"有学上"到"上好学"的转变，同时强调了教师在价值观引导和情感交流中的不可替代作用。

A Historic Transformation in Education

Inside a math classroom at Beijing No. 13 Secondary School, an artificial intelligence-powered "agent" crunches weeks of student data, analyzing students' recent classroom participation, homework and quiz results. Minutes later, a teacher reviews and refines AI-generated question sequences tailored for students at different learning levels and personalized after-class assignments before class can begin.

This scene reflects a broader transformation taking place in the world's largest basic education system — which serves more than 220 million students — as China turns to AI to improve the quality of basic education and better meet the needs of individual students, following decades of expanding access to education and narrowing regional disparities.

From Access to Quality

Having largely achieved universal enrollment in compulsory education, China is shifting its focus from ensuring access to schooling for all children toward providing every child with higher-quality, more personalized education. The country's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-30) calls for leveraging AI to transform educational models and integrating the technology into every element, stage and scenario of education.

In April, the Ministry of Education launched an "AI Plus Education" action plan to accelerate AI education in primary and secondary schools, encourage interdisciplinary teaching and expand AI applications in classrooms. These policies are already taking shape nationwide.

Innovative Practices Across China

In Wenzhou, East China's Zhejiang province, AI literacy classes were introduced, through which students created digital avatars that introduce the city's culture in the local dialect using generative AI. By analyzing learning data, the AI "agent" at Beijing No. 13 Secondary School not only identifies common learning gaps among students, but also pinpoints the academic strengths and weaknesses of each one.

For instance, Zhang Meiya, a teacher at the school, worked with the AI system to shift abstract content closer to students' daily lives, developing case studies on issues such as smart-home privacy disputes and the spread of AI-generated fake news, before teaching a lesson on citizens' rights and responsibilities.

Teachers' Roles Evolving

At Beijing No. 35 High School, programming teacher Dong Yue has seen another benefit. "Previously, we had to examine each student's code line by line, which took a tremendous amount of time and effort. Now the AI agent can complete the initial assessment very quickly," Dong said. The time saved is now devoted to lesson planning, individualized guidance and professional development instead of repetitive grading.

However, teachers warn that AI remains far from perfect. Current systems may struggle to accurately evaluate certain types of writing, such as travel essays, or assess students' reasoning processes in some mathematics problems. Data interoperability between different AI platforms also remains a challenge in some areas.

The Irreplaceable Role of Teachers

"That is precisely why the role of teachers has not been diminished but rather strengthened," said Wu Chunhui, vice-principal of Beijing No. 13 Secondary School. "AI is an assisting tool. It cannot replace teachers in providing values-based guidance, inspiring students intellectually, or engaging empathetically with students in complex situations."

Liu Jizhong, principal of Beijing No. 35 High School, believes teachers will increasingly become designers of learning experiences, gatekeepers of AI use and guardians of technological ethics, rather than simply transmitters of knowledge.

As Yao Xiaoying, principal of the Foreign Languages Primary School affiliated with the Shenzhen University Education Group, put it, "Technology is merely the fuse. What we truly want to ignite is every child's inner motivation, which is the confidence to say, 'I want to learn, and I can do it.'"

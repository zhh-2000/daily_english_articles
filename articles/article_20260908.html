Heritage at Risk: European Heritage Days 2026 Rally Communities to Save Endangered Culture

📅 September 8, 2026  |  📖 约980词  |  ⏱️ 建议阅读时间：15分钟

📋 文章摘要

2026年9月3日，欧洲文化遗产日（European Heritage Days）正式启动，覆盖50多个欧洲国家，将持续至10月初。今年主题为"濒危遗产：复兴、抵抗、重塑"，聚焦气候变化、武装冲突、快速城市化和传统生活方式消亡对文化遗产构成的严峻威胁。在西班牙和匈牙利，导览活动聚焦塑造外交与和平的中世纪女性；布鲁塞尔将废弃啤酒厂改造为沉浸式文化空间；克罗地亚社区重新发现"圣徒守卫"传统。与此同时，欧洲委员会为乌克兰开放文化遗产损害赔偿新类别。该活动自1985年始于法国，1999年起由欧洲委员会与欧盟联合举办，强调社区在遗产保护中的核心作用。

🏛️ A Continent Mobilises for Culture

On September 3, 2026, the European Heritage Days (EHD) kicked off across more than 50 European countries, running through to early October. Launched in 1985 in France and organised jointly by the Council of Europe and the European Union since 1999, the initiative is the most widely celebrated participatory cultural event in Europe. Each year, participating countries organise their programmes around a specific theme, offering citizens fresh perspectives on the history of the people and places that have enriched European culture and heritage.

This year's theme, "Heritage at Risk: Revive, Resist, Reimagine," confronts one of the continent's most urgent challenges: protecting cultural heritage that is endangered. Through thousands of events — including guided tours, exhibitions, workshops, performances, and digital experiences — communities across Europe are showcasing how heritage at risk can be safeguarded and passed on to future generations.

"European Heritage Days celebrate what makes Europe distinctive and diverse," said Glenn Micallef, Commissioner for Intergenerational Fairness, Youth, Culture and Sport. "This year's theme reminds us that heritage threatened by war, climate change, disasters or neglect needs active protection and that communities have a crucial role in keeping it alive for future generations."

⚠️ Threats from Every Direction

Europe's cultural treasures are increasingly vulnerable to a constellation of threats. Climate change and environmental disasters are eroding ancient monuments and historic sites at an unprecedented rate. Conflicts have inflicted devastating damage on irreplaceable cultural assets. Rapid urban development is swallowing traditional neighbourhoods, while the loss of traditional ways of life threatens the intangible heritage — languages, crafts, rituals, and knowledge — that gives communities their identity.

The most stark example is Russia's war of aggression against Ukraine, which has damaged or destroyed hundreds of the country's cultural-heritage sites. In response, the Council of Europe's Register of Damage for Ukraine has opened a new category of claims covering damage, destruction, or loss of objects or buildings of cultural value. Ukrainian state and legal entities can now submit these claims with a view to receiving compensation in the future — a landmark step in linking cultural heritage protection to accountability and justice.

Beyond conflict, the threats are multifaceted. Rising sea levels threaten coastal archaeological sites. Intense rainfall and flooding damage medieval buildings whose foundations were never designed to withstand such conditions. Mass tourism, while economically beneficial, places enormous physical strain on fragile sites. Even digitisation, paradoxically, poses risks: as communities rush to digitise their heritage, questions arise about who controls the digital copies and whether the original artefacts are neglected once a virtual version exists.

🌟 Revive, Resist, Reimagine

The three pillars of this year's theme offer a roadmap for action. "Revive" calls for breathing new life into neglected or abandoned heritage sites. In Brussels, an immersive experience at a former brewery demonstrates how industrial heritage can be reimagined through artistic creation and community participation, transforming a derelict space into a vibrant cultural hub. In the French Ardennes, exhibitions showcasing over 1,000 minerals and fossils are reviving interest in the region's geological heritage.

"Resist" highlights the importance of defending heritage against destructive forces. In Croatia, participatory activities invite local communities to rediscover the tradition of the Saints' Guard through the documentation and interpretation of roadside chapels and devotional monuments — ensuring that a centuries-old practice is not lost to modernisation and secularisation. In Spain and Hungary, coordinated guided heritage visits explore the lives of medieval women who shaped diplomacy and peacebuilding, connecting historic sites across Europe through a shared narrative of governance, dialogue, and cultural exchange.

"Reimagine" encourages creative approaches to heritage protection. The Faro Convention, which underpins the entire initiative, recognises the value of cultural heritage for society and emphasises the crucial role of communities in identifying, safeguarding, and transmitting heritage. By encouraging people to engage with the heritage they value, European Heritage Days strengthen democratic participation, social cohesion, and intercultural dialogue across Europe.

🌍 A Global Movement

The spirit of the European Heritage Days resonates far beyond the continent. Around the world, institutions and communities are leveraging innovative approaches to safeguard cultural heritage. In China, the Dunhuang Academy has employed artificial intelligence and advanced digital tools to preserve the millennia-old Mogao Grottoes, a UNESCO World Heritage site since 1987. AI models trained on tens of thousands of high-resolution images achieve "pixel-level" disease detection, identifying early-stage damage thinner than a human hair, while the "Digital Library Cave" platform has released digital resources of over 11,000 manuscripts and artifacts, attracting more than 250,000 visits from 33 countries and regions.

In September 2026, the National Copyright Administration of China and the World Intellectual Property Organization co-hosted an international symposium in Fuzhou on copyright protection and the promotion of folk arts, exploring how to safeguard cultural roots in the age of artificial intelligence. The discussion underscored a truth that the European Heritage Days also embody: traditional culture is not merely a relic of the past but a living, evolving inheritance that requires active stewardship.

🔄 Heritage as a Living Bond

The European Heritage Days are known by many names across the continent — Tag des Denkmals in Austria, Journées du Patrimoine in France, Open Monumentendag in Belgium and the Netherlands, Kulturális Örökség Napjai in Hungary, Heritage Open Days in the United Kingdom. This diversity of names reflects a shared commitment: the belief that cultural heritage, whether tangible or intangible, is a bridge between generations and a foundation for democratic participation, social cohesion, and intercultural dialogue.

As Commissioner Micallef noted, the theme serves as a reminder that heritage "needs active protection." The events across Europe this autumn are not merely celebrations; they are acts of resistance against erasure, acts of imagination toward renewal, and acts of revival for what has been neglected. In a world where conflict, climate change, and rapid development conspire to erase the past, the European Heritage Days stand as a testament to the power of communities to remember, protect, and reimagine the cultural inheritance that defines who we are.

🔍 长难句解析

① Through thousands of events — including guided tours, exhibitions, workshops, performances, and digital experiences — communities across Europe are showcasing how heritage at risk can be safeguarded and passed on to future generations.
结构分析：此句为主从复合句。句首"Through thousands of events"是介词短语作方式状语，其中破折号之间的"including guided tours, exhibitions, workshops, performances, and digital experiences"是介词短语作补充说明（including引导列举，五个名词由逗号和and连接）。主句主语是"communities"，"across Europe"是介词短语作后置定语修饰communities，谓语是"are showcasing"（现在进行时），宾语是how引导的宾语从句"how heritage at risk can be safeguarded and passed on to future generations"。在宾语从句中，"heritage at risk"是主语（at risk是介词短语作后置定语），"can be safeguarded and passed on"是并列被动谓语（由and连接），"to future generations"是介词短语作状语。该句通过方式状语、宾语从句和并列谓语，完整描述了欧洲文化遗产日的行动方式与目标。
翻译：通过数以千计的活动——包括导览参观、展览、工作坊、表演和数字体验——欧洲各地的社区正在展示如何保护濒危遗产并将其传承给后代。
② In response, the Council of Europe's Register of Damage for Ukraine has opened a new category of claims covering damage, destruction, or loss of objects or buildings of cultural value.
结构分析：此句为简单句扩展结构。"In response"是介词短语作状语（意为"作为回应"）。主语是"the Council of Europe's Register of Damage for Ukraine"（由多个名词和介词短语构成，Register是中心词，其余为定语），谓语是"has opened"（现在完成时），宾语是"a new category of claims"（of claims是介词短语作后置定语修饰category）。其后"covering damage, destruction, or loss of objects or buildings of cultural value"是现在分词短语作后置定语修饰claims（covering意为"涵盖"），其中"damage, destruction, or loss"是三个并列名词作covering的宾语，"of objects or buildings"是介词短语作后置定语修饰damage, destruction, or loss，"of cultural value"是介词短语作后置定语修饰objects or buildings。该句通过多重后置定语，精确描述了索赔类别的覆盖范围。
翻译：作为回应，欧洲委员会的乌克兰损害登记册开放了一个新的索赔类别，涵盖具有文化价值的物品或建筑的损害、毁坏或丢失。
③ The Faro Convention, which underpins the entire initiative, recognises the value of cultural heritage for society and emphasises the crucial role of communities in identifying, safeguarding, and transmitting heritage.
结构分析：此句为主从复合句。主句主语是"The Faro Convention"，其后"which underpins the entire initiative"是非限制性定语从句修饰The Faro Convention（which在从句中作主语，underpins是谓语，the entire initiative是宾语）。主句有两个并列谓语：第一个是"recognises"，宾语是"the value of cultural heritage for society"（of cultural heritage和for society都是介词短语作后置定语修饰value）；第二个谓语是"emphasises"（由and连接），宾语是"the crucial role of communities in identifying, safeguarding, and transmitting heritage"（of communities是介词短语作后置定语修饰role，"in identifying, safeguarding, and transmitting heritage"是介词短语作后置定语修饰role，其中三个动名词由逗号和and连接作in的宾语）。该句通过非限制性定语从句和并列谓语，全面阐述了法罗公约的核心原则。
翻译：支撑整个倡议的《法罗公约》认可文化遗产对社会的价值，并强调社区在识别、保护和传承遗产中的关键作用。
④ In a world where conflict, climate change, and rapid development conspire to erase the past, the European Heritage Days stand as a testament to the power of communities to remember, protect, and reimagine the cultural inheritance that defines who we are.
结构分析：此句为主从复合句，结构较为复杂。句首"In a world"是介词短语作地点状语，其后"where conflict, climate change, and rapid development conspire to erase the past"是where引导的定语从句修饰world（where在从句中作地点状语，"conflict, climate change, and rapid development"是并列主语，conspire是谓语，"to erase the past"是不定式作宾语/目的状语）。主句主语是"the European Heritage Days"，谓语是"stand"，"as a testament"是介词短语作表语/状语，"to the power of communities"是介词短语作后置定语修饰testament，"to remember, protect, and reimagine the cultural inheritance"是不定式短语作后置定语修饰power（三个动词由逗号和and连接）。最后"that defines who we are"是that引导的定语从句修饰cultural inheritance（that在从句中作主语，defines是谓语，"who we are"是who引导的宾语从句作defines的宾语）。该句通过定语从句嵌套、不定式短语和宾语从句，构建了一个气势恢宏的总结性陈述。
翻译：在冲突、气候变化和快速发展共同试图抹去过去的世界中，欧洲文化遗产日证明了社区在记忆、保护和重塑定义我们身份的文化遗产方面的力量。

💬 思考与讨论

Question 1: The European Heritage Days 2026 theme identifies climate change, conflicts, rapid development, and the loss of traditional ways of life as the main threats to cultural heritage. Which of these threats do you think is the most difficult to address, and why? Using evidence from the article — such as the damage to Ukraine's cultural sites or the erosion of coastal archaeological areas — discuss whether international cooperation or local community action is more effective in protecting endangered heritage.
Question 2: The article describes how the three pillars — "Revive, Resist, Reimagine" — offer a roadmap for heritage protection. The Brussels brewery project and the Croatian Saints' Guard documentation are examples of reviving and resisting, respectively. In your view, what does it mean to "reimagine" cultural heritage? Should technology such as AI and VR be embraced as tools for preservation, or do they risk replacing authentic experiences with virtual substitutes? Consider the example of the Mogao Grottoes' digital preservation in your response.
Question 3: The Faro Convention emphasises "the crucial role of communities in identifying, safeguarding, and transmitting heritage," and the article notes that European Heritage Days "strengthen democratic participation, social cohesion, and intercultural dialogue." Why is community involvement considered so essential to heritage protection? Discuss how involving local communities in heritage preservation might differ from a top-down, government-led approach, and what challenges communities might face when competing against rapid urban development or mass tourism.

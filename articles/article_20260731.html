Silicon-Based Quantum Chips: A Major Step Toward Practical Quantum Computing

📅 July 31, 2026 📂 Science & Technology 📖 Difficulty: Intermediate-Advanced ⏱️ Reading Time: 10 min

📝 文章摘要

国际顶级学术期刊《自然》最新发表的两项独立研究表明，硅基量子计算芯片已能执行对实现量子计算至关重要的基本运算，标志着实用化量子计算机研发迈出重要一步。美国HRL实验室和荷兰代尔夫特理工大学的研究团队分别在不同技术路径上取得突破，为解决量子比特规模化集成与稳定控制难题提供了新思路。

Full Article

Quantum computing has long promised to revolutionize fields ranging from drug discovery to financial modeling. However, building practical quantum computers that can outperform classical systems has proven extraordinarily difficult. Two groundbreaking studies published in Nature on July 29, 2026, now bring that dream significantly closer to reality.

Both research teams independently demonstrated that silicon-based quantum computing chips can execute fundamental operations essential for practical quantum computation. This achievement is particularly significant because silicon—the same material used in traditional computer chips—offers the potential for mass production and integration with existing semiconductor manufacturing processes.

The first study, conducted by researchers at HRL Laboratories in the United States, addressed one of the most persistent challenges in quantum computing: heat management. Quantum bits, or qubits, are extremely sensitive to temperature changes. Traditional silicon-based quantum processors require numerous external wires connecting to control circuits, and these connections generate heat that can destroy the fragile quantum information stored in qubits.

"By integrating control electronics directly within the silicon-based quantum processor, we reduced heat transfer to sensitive qubits, enabling repeated error correction during computation."

The HRL team's innovation was to place the electronic control systems inside the same ultra-cold environment as the quantum processor itself. Their prototype, fabricated by commercial semiconductor manufacturers, successfully operated with up to 18 qubits. Remarkably, they observed quantum error correction protocols functioning across up to seven qubits simultaneously—a crucial capability for building reliable quantum computers.

The second study, led by researchers at Delft University of Technology in the Netherlands, explored a different approach to another fundamental challenge: communication between distant qubits. Their design employs a mobile qubit that travels between four stationary qubits, functioning like a "bus" in classical computing architecture.

This "quantum bus" approach addresses a critical limitation in current quantum processors. As quantum chips grow larger, connecting distant qubits becomes increasingly difficult. The Dutch team's solution allows qubits separated by significant distances to interact and perform joint operations, essential for large-scale quantum computation.

The significance of using silicon as the base material cannot be overstated. Unlike alternative quantum computing platforms that require exotic materials or completely new manufacturing infrastructure, silicon-based quantum processors can potentially be produced using existing semiconductor fabrication facilities. This could dramatically accelerate the path from laboratory prototypes to mass-produced quantum chips.

However, substantial challenges remain. Current silicon-based quantum processors still contain relatively few qubits compared to other technologies. Google's Willow processor, for instance, operates with 105 qubits using superconducting technology. IBM's latest Nighthawk processor features 120 qubits. Both companies are pursuing aggressive roadmaps to reach thousands of qubits within the next few years.

The competition extends beyond Western companies. China has made quantum technology a strategic priority, establishing a technical committee on quantum information standardization under the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology on July 31, 2026. This body will develop industry standards across quantum computing, communication, and precision measurement, strengthening China's position in this critical emerging field.

Chinese researchers have also reported significant achievements. In May 2026, scientists developed "Jiuzhang 4.0," a programmable quantum computing prototype that set a world record in optical quantum information technology. At the World Artificial Intelligence Conference earlier in July, a Chinese team unveiled the ShanghaiCube Pro, a hybrid quantum-classical computing system featuring 2,048 stable physical quantum bits.

Industry experts note that silicon-based quantum computing complements rather than replaces other approaches. Each technology platform—superconducting, trapped ion, photonic, and silicon-based—has distinct advantages for different applications. Silicon's particular strength lies in its compatibility with classical computing infrastructure, making it attractive for near-term hybrid systems.

The Nature publications prompted enthusiastic responses from the scientific community. A companion "News and Views" article noted that while whether silicon-based quantum processors can replicate traditional semiconductor chips' dramatic rise remains uncertain, these studies highlight that building practical quantum computers is as much an engineering challenge as a physics problem.

For students preparing for exams, understanding quantum computing basics offers valuable context for technology-related reading passages. Key concepts include superposition (qubits existing in multiple states simultaneously), entanglement (correlated quantum states across separated particles), and quantum error correction (detecting and correcting errors without destroying quantum information).

As research progresses across multiple technology platforms worldwide, the quantum computing revolution appears increasingly inevitable. The silicon-based breakthroughs announced this week may well mark the moment when quantum computing began its transition from laboratory curiosity to practical technology.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

1. "Both research teams independently demonstrated that silicon-based quantum computing chips can execute fundamental operations essential for practical quantum computation."
句子结构分析：
• 主语：Both research teams
• 谓语：demonstrated
• 宾语从句：that silicon-based quantum computing chips can execute fundamental operations
• 后置定语：essential for practical quantum computation (修饰operations)
难点：宾语从句中包含形容词短语作后置定语，造成理解障碍。
2. "The HRL team's innovation was to place the electronic control systems inside the same ultra-cold environment as the quantum processor itself."
句子结构分析：
• 主语：The HRL team's innovation
• 系动词：was
• 表语：to place... (不定式短语作表语)
• 地点状语：inside the same ultra-cold environment as the quantum processor itself
难点：不定式作表语，以及"the same...as"比较结构的理解。
3. "Unlike alternative quantum computing platforms that require exotic materials or completely new manufacturing infrastructure, silicon-based quantum processors can potentially be produced using existing semiconductor fabrication facilities."
句子结构分析：
• 介词短语：Unlike alternative quantum computing platforms... (作状语)
• 定语从句：that require exotic materials or... (修饰platforms)
• 主句主语：silicon-based quantum processors
• 谓语：can potentially be produced
• 方式状语：using existing semiconductor fabrication facilities
难点：句首长介词短语内嵌定语从句，造成主句延迟出现。
4. "A companion 'News and Views' article noted that while whether silicon-based quantum processors can replicate traditional semiconductor chips' dramatic rise remains uncertain, these studies highlight that building practical quantum computers is as much an engineering challenge as a physics problem."
句子结构分析：
• 主句：A companion article noted that...
• 第一层宾语从句：that while..., these studies highlight that...
• while引导的让步状语从句：whether... remains uncertain
• whether引导的主语从句：whether silicon-based quantum processors can replicate...
• highlight后的宾语从句：that building... is as much...as...
难点：多层从句嵌套，including while让步状语从句中的whether主语从句。

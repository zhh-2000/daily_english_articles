Sodium-Ion Batteries Enter Mass Production Era

July 28, 2026 | Source: Science Daily
Technology • Energy • Environment

【文章摘要】

钠离子电池正迈入大规模生产阶段，有望在电动汽车和储能领域挑战锂离子电池。钠资源储量丰富、成本低廉，且在极寒环境下性能优异。宁德时代等中国企业正引领这一技术革命，能量密度已与磷酸铁锂电池相当，为构建更清洁、更经济的能源生态系统带来新希望。

A New Challenger Emerges

For decades, lithium-ion batteries have powered our world—from 8 billion smartphones to millions of electric vehicles. However, a powerful challenger is emerging: sodium-ion batteries. This year may mark the beginning of the sodium-ion battery era, as this technology rapidly moves toward mass production.

The advantages of sodium are compelling. Sodium is abundant in the Earth's crust, with its abundance being over 1,000 times greater than lithium. It can be extracted from common rock salt and seawater, making it significantly cheaper and more accessible. Industry analysts predict that by the end of 2026, sodium-ion batteries will match the cost of lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries, with even greater cost advantages expected by 2027.

Breaking the Cold Barrier

Cold weather has always been lithium-ion batteries' weakness. In freezing conditions, lithium electrolytes become inefficient—charging slows down, and available capacity drops significantly. Sodium-ion technology, however, offers a remarkable solution to this problem.

Testing shows that sodium batteries maintain over 85% capacity at temperatures as low as -40°C. This exceptional low-temperature performance makes them particularly attractive for electric vehicles operating in cold regions. In February 2026, Chinese automaker Changan partnered with CATL to launch a sodium-ion battery vehicle, specifically marketing its reliability in extreme cold weather.

Durability Meets Sustainability

Modern sodium battery packs demonstrate impressive durability, enduring over 10,000 complete charge-discharge cycles before capacity drops to 80%. For electric vehicles, this translates to millions of kilometers of driving. In many cases, the battery pack outlasts the vehicle itself, dramatically reducing replacement costs and improving long-term ownership value.

Beyond vehicles, sodium-ion batteries are becoming the foundation for grid energy storage. Their lower cost, higher safety, and extended service life make them ideal for large-scale energy storage facilities. These installations help balance renewable energy fluctuations and reduce dependence on lithium resources. CATL has developed a sodium-based storage platform designed to last 25-30 years.

The Energy Density Challenge

While sodium-ion batteries excel in cost and safety, energy density remains their primary challenge. Current mass-produced sodium batteries achieve 160-175 Wh/kg, comparable to LFP batteries but still about two-thirds of advanced lithium-ion technology.

Researchers worldwide are working to close this gap. CATL's latest technology aims to push energy density to 200 Wh/kg. Innovative approaches, including "anode-free" designs where sodium deposits directly as metal during charging, could further revolutionize this technology. Some experts believe certain sodium-ion configurations could eventually rival lithium-ion batteries in performance.

A Diversified Energy Future

The future energy market will not depend on a single battery type. Lithium batteries will continue serving premium electric vehicles demanding extreme range and compact design. Sodium-ion batteries will rapidly become the preferred choice for economical vehicles, urban transportation, renewable energy storage, and applications in extreme weather conditions.

As mass production continues advancing, sodium-ion batteries are positioned to become a crucial engine for building cleaner, more economical, and more resilient energy ecosystems. This technology is reshaping the landscape of electric vehicles and global energy infrastructure, offering a sustainable path toward our energy future.
